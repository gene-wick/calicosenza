The Wolves Come Home at Dawn: Cosenza, Inter's Children, and a Season Read From the Bottom of the Boot

Published November 1, 2026

The Lupi return to the San Vito–Gigi Marulla after a summer and autumn in exile, against a giant's reserve side, at 3:30 in the morning California time. A diaspora desk's season-so-far, with Mbappé, Olise, Haaland and the rest of the pantheon viewed through a Calabrian lens.

Abstract (italiano): Domenica 1 novembre, alle 12:30, il Cosenza torna al San Vito–Gigi Marulla dopo quasi tre mesi di esilio allo Scida di Crotone, contro l'Inter U23 di Stefano Vecchi, nella 12ª giornata del Girone C. I Lupi arrivano penultimi con 6 punti in 11 gare, una sola vittoria (a Picerno) e una classifica che, secondo mister Coppitelli, non rispecchia le prestazioni. Questo pezzo legge la stagione europea fino a fine ottobre dal Mezzogiorno: il derby di Milano, le italiane in Champions, la Francia di Zidane fermata dall'Italia, e i grandi interpreti che seguiamo dalla California, da Mbappé a Kane. Parliamo anche di chi paga davvero il calcio: lo stadio è pubblico, il contenzioso è privato, e il biglietto lo paga sempre il tifoso. Note live su @calicosenza e analisi completa dopo il fischio finale su calicosenza.com.

Voice note: This desk writes as a respectful outsider in California — honoring Calabrian family heritage and doing the homework — not as a member of the curva.

I. The hour the clocks gave back

The scheduling of this one is cruel and kind at once. Cosenza against Inter Under 23 kicks off at 12:30 in Italy on Sunday, November 1, which on the Pacific coast is 3:30 in the morning. But the American clocks fall back at 2:00 that same night, so the rossoblù faithful of California’s Central Coast receive, by some bureaucratic act of providence, one extra hour of sleep. We will waste it lying awake at 1:58 thinking about the defensive line. Still, the universe made a gesture, and this season has not been generous with anything else.

The headline is simple and not at all small: the Lupi are going home. After almost three months of "home" fixtures played at the Ezio Scida in Crotone, a forced exile caused by renovation works at the San Vito–Gigi Marulla and a legal dispute between the club and the Comune, the municipal vigilance commission granted the stadium its agibilità for this match, capped at 5,000 spectators. The club's own note says the Curva Sud "Denis Bergamini," the Curva Nord "Massimiliano Catena," the Tribuna Rao and the uncovered Tribuna B stay closed, and that those limitations derive from "decisions not attributable to the will of the club." Tribuna A, the Rossa sectors, the Centrale and the away end are open. The curves, the heart of any southern ground, named for the dead the city carries, stay dark for the homecoming.

The football context is, to put it diplomatically, a crime scene. Cosenza sit nineteenth of twenty in Girone C: 6 points from 11 matches, one win, three draws, seven defeats, nine goals scored and twenty-three conceded. The only side below Cosenza is Crotone, hosts in exile, who sit on 1 point because of a six-point penalty. Without that penalty the Lupi would be last. The first win of the season arrived at Picerno on October 12, a 2–1 comeback built from Achour's equaliser and McJannet's go-ahead goal, nine matchdays into the campaign. Six days later came a 3–1 defeat to Team Altamura, played behind closed doors in Crotone. Then a 1–1 at Savoia on October 24, where Pascalau scored on 66 minutes and the hosts equalised within about a minute. That was the fourth point in five matches. In this league, at that address, that counts as form.

Federico Coppitelli, at his pre-match press conference on Saturday, made two points worth recording exactly. On the table: "Nella classifica attuale mancano almeno 3/4 punti per le prestazioni che sono state prodotte." In other words, the current table is short at least three or four points relative to what the performances deserved. On the opponent: "le compagini Under-23 non sono squadre di Serie C, sono dei collettivi differenti." Under-23 sides are not Serie C teams, they are different kinds of collectives. He also thanked Crotone FC for its hospitality. Calabrian football solidarity is rarer than it should be, so it deserves writing down.

Inter Under 23, coached by Stefano Vecchi, arrive ninth on 17 points from 11 matches, a game in hand on most of the group. On Tuesday they beat Scafatese 2–1 in a recovered fixture, with Jan Zuberek opening the scoring and Marello deciding it from a free kick. Zuberek has six league goals per the Lega Pro scorer list. Their season reads like a youth project's should: 2–1 winners at Catania on the opening day, 4–1 losers at Picerno, 4–2 losers at home to Monopoli. Talented, volatile, well coached. Coppitelli is right that they are a different animal.

II. Who pays for the stadium? (A short materialist interlude)

The San Vito–Gigi Marulla is public property. The works that sent us to Crotone are a municipal renovation project. In July, the club's president, Eugenio Guarascio, took the Comune to the TAR of Calabria, contesting the declaration of inagibilità and asking, as a fallback, for damages of at least €1,008,911, a figure the filing reportedly ties to extraordinary maintenance the club says it paid for over the years. The supporters, meanwhile, have spent the autumn contesting the ownership itself. A public asset, a private concession, a lawsuit between them, and in the middle the people who own neither, whose loyalty is the only thing worth arguing over.

And then the return ticket. Gazzetta del Sud reports the homecoming came with a price rise: Tribuna A at €19, the Rossa sectors at €29, the Centrale at €50, with the club saying the tariffs were "adjusted according to indications provided by the Lega Pro." The box office takes electronic payment only. So the fan who travelled to Crotone for "home" games now pays more to sit in a partly closed ground they already own as citizens. This is not about one individual; it is the structure. The stadium is public property, the club and the Comune are in court over at least €1,008,911 in claimed damages, and the bill for the homecoming — higher ticket prices, electronic payment only — lands on the person at the turnstile.

Now consider the opponent. The B-team model, which Serie C has opened to the giants' Under-23 sides, lets a club like Inter run its development pipeline through a professional league full of provincial clubs that live and die by the gate. Inter U23 play their home games at the Stadio Breda in Sesto San Giovanni, a working-class industrial suburb north of Milan. There is an irony history would appreciate: that suburb now hosts the reserve side of a global brand, and the reserve side comes south to a city whose own club cannot get its curves open. None of this is the Inter kids' fault; they are young workers learning a trade. But one institution is a laboratory and the other is a community, and only one of them truly suffers if it is relegated.

III. The view from San Siro, and from the bottom of the boot

A diaspora supporter watches football the way an emigrant reads news from home: filtered through distance, weighed against the village. Here is the season as it looks from a California kitchen at strange hours.

Serie A. The headline from Saturday night is the Derby della Madonnina, which Milan won 3–1 at San Siro with all four goals in the first half: Pulisic headed in on 4 minutes, Rabiot finished off the post on 10, Alessandro Bastoni pulled one back from a corner on 22, and Diego Moreira, who set up the first two, put a free kick over the wall on 43. Inter, unbeaten in the league until then, stay on 25 points but drop below Lazio on goal difference. Amorim's Milan move to 21. Lazio, also on 25, host Cagliari on Sunday with the chance to go top alone. Roma were third on 21 going into the weekend. Como were fourth on 20, and Frosinone sixth on 17, the provincial story this desk adores, with Raimondo level with Monza's Varela on eight league goals. Juventus, level with Napoli on 14 after losing 2–0 at Genoa, host them on Sunday night. At the bottom, Fiorentina, Udinese and Parma are on 5 and Venezia on 3. The drop does not care about pedigree. Cosenza fans know.

The Champions League. Four Italian clubs are in the league phase, and after three matchdays the picture is mixed. Como sat ninth on 6 points after beating Manchester United 4–1, a sentence I will never tire of typing. Roma were fifteenth on 4 after a 4–0 against Slovan Bratislava. Inter had 3 points in twentieth after a 1–0 over Shakhtar, and Napoli had 3 in twenty-second after a 4–3 against Bodø/Glimt. At the top, holders Paris Saint-Germain led on 9 points, level with Aston Villa, Liverpool, Real Madrid and Real Betis. Matchday four is November 3–4.

France, Italy, and Bastoni again. The international thread runs straight through Italian football. On October 2 at the Stade de France, Zinédine Zidane's first home match as sélectionneur ended France 1, Italy 1 in the Nations League. Michael Olise curled a free kick into the top corner on 55 minutes, and Alessandro Bastoni, the same Bastoni who scored in the derby, intercepted a loose pass and equalised on 68. Donnarumma, the former Paris goalkeeper, frustrated Les Bleus all night, and Upamecano was sent off late. Three days later France came from behind to beat Belgium 4–1, with substitutes Doué, Cherki and Olise turning it and Olise scoring twice at the death. The return fixture, Italy against France, is at San Siro on November 12. For a household that loves Les Bleus and lives inside Italian football, that is the circled date of the month.

That is all happening after a World Cup in which France finished fourth: beaten 2–0 by Spain in the semi-final, then beaten 6–4 by England in the third-place match. Spain won the tournament, beating Argentina 1–0 in the final.

IV. The pantheon, refracted

I have a ranking, defended at family tables from San Francisco to the Sila. Here are the seven, order intact.

Kylian Mbappé remains the sun around which the rest of us orbit. At the World Cup he scored ten goals, became the first player to win the Golden Boot twice, and moved to 22 career World Cup goals, a tournament record. On Monday at the London Palladium he finished second in the Ballon d'Or by 28 points, 1,027 to 1,055, one of the narrowest margins in the award's history. The day before, at the Camp Nou, he had already filed his rebuttal: two goals in Madrid's 2–1 Clásico win on October 25, the first a fortunate deflection on 41 minutes, the second an 87th-minute winner into an empty net. That gave him eleven league goals, second only to Raphinha's thirteen. Mourinho said he saw "class. And respect" in how Mbappé handled the vote. For once, Mourinho is right.

Michael Olise is the player I would pay my own ticket price to watch, if anyone were selling them in Munich at Calabrian rates. By October 28, after two goals and an assist in a 6–1 cup win over Magdeburg, he had twelve goals in all competitions, per Stats Foot via Goal, overtaking Kane as Bayern's top scorer. He finished ninth in the Ballon d'Or voting and reached nine goals in 28 caps for France with that free kick against Italy, before adding two more against Belgium. A left foot like that is a public good.

Erling Haaland has thirteen goals in twelve appearances, including a brace in a 4–2 home defeat to PSG. City lost 1–0 at home to Brighton on Saturday but stay top of the Premier League on 21 points, level with Arsenal, who have played a game fewer. The Guardiola era is over, Enzo Maresca is in charge, and the Norwegian is the only constant.

Jude Bellingham has been reworked by Mourinho as the 10 in a 4-2-3-1, freer near the box and less burdened with chasing. In the Clásico it was his skill on halfway that started the move for the winner, Camavinga carrying it on and Mbappé finishing. Madrid are third, three points behind Barcelona, and play at Racing Santander on Sunday.

Ousmane Dembélé captained France from the start for the first time against Italy, on his 70th cap, and scored in PSG's win at the Etihad on October 14. Then he hurt his left thigh against Barcelona on October 20; Le Parisien estimated three weeks out. He finished seventh in the Ballon d'Or. PSG have kept rolling without him: a 5–1 at Le Havre on Friday, with an Achraf Hakimi hat-trick, puts them top of Ligue 1 on 20.

Bradley Barcola is now a Liverpool player, having moved from PSG at the end of the summer window. He told the club website that the Premier League is "very intense. It's very different from Ligue 1." Iraola plays him on both wings, though he prefers the left. He started for France against Belgium and was close to an equaliser before Doué came on. A young man learning a new shift in a new country: the diaspora sees itself in that.

Harry Kane is the reigning Ballon d'Or winner, the first Englishman since Michael Owen in 2001, after 73 goals in 65 matches last season and a Bundesliga and German Cup double. Bayern top the Bundesliga on goal difference over Dortmund, both on 18 after eight matches. He sits seventh in my ranking and first in Europe's ballot, and I am at peace with that contradiction. Rankings are about love; ballots are about labour. He has done the labour.

Thierry Henry, the old-school patron saint of this desk, co-hosted that Ballon d'Or night in London with Kate Scott. It is worth recalling, for an Italian audience, that Serie A once had Henry and did not understand him: Juventus signed him in January 1999 and played him wide, and by the summer he was at Arsenal becoming the most elegant centre-forward of his generation. Italian football often misreads what is in front of it. Cosenza fans recognise the pattern.

V. Back to the spear tip

Sunday at dawn in California, lunchtime on the Crati. What to watch?

First, the ground itself. The San Vito–Gigi Marulla with 5,000 people in it is a different organism from a closed Scida in Crotone, and Coppitelli is openly counting on "un briciolo di entusiasmo," a crumb of enthusiasm from outside.

Second, the margins Coppitelli keeps naming. If his reading is right, and the side has played better than nine goals for and twenty-three against suggests, the gap is in the details: the minutes after scoring and conceding, set pieces, game management. The squad's goal threat has been spread thin, with McJannet on two league goals by mid-October and Achour, Dalmazzi and Pascalau chipping in, so a set piece may matter more than usual.

Third, the opponent's volatility. Inter U23 have conceded nineteen goals in eleven matches. They are good enough to beat anyone in this group and young enough to lose concentration against anyone. Zuberek is the one to watch. Marello's free kick on Tuesday is a warning about fouls on the edge of the box.

Fourth, the table. Direct safety, fifteenth place, is currently Casarano on 14 points. That is eight points from where Cosenza stand. A home win on reopening day would at least turn the conversation from mourning to arithmetic. Elsewhere in the group on Sunday, Potenza, top on 26, host Crotone at 17:30. You can guess which side of that one the Calabrian solidarity is on.
